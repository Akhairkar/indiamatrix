India's Changing Population

How growth, ageing, and shifting demographics are reshaping the country's future.

IM

IndiaMetrix Editorial

August 2026 • 3 min read

India is now the most populous country in the world, having surpassed China. But beneath this headline figure lies a more complex and fascinating story of demographic transition. The narrative is shifting from rapid, unbridled growth to stabilization and, eventually, an ageing population.

The Long Arc of Growth

Looking at historical data from the World Bank, India's population has grown steadily since the 1960s. In 1960, India's population was roughly 445 million. Today, it stands at over 1.45 billion. This remarkable growth was fueled by massive improvements in public health, reductions in infant mortality, and increased life expectancy.

The Rate of Growth is Slowing

While the total numbers are staggering, the percentage growth rate has actually been declining for decades. The Total Fertility Rate (TFR) — the average number of children a woman is expected to have — has fallen below the replacement level of 2.1 in a majority of Indian states. This means that, in the long term, India's population will eventually peak and begin to decline, mirroring the trajectory of East Asian and Western nations.

The Demographic Dividend

Currently, India is experiencing a 'demographic dividend' — a period where the working-age population is significantly larger than the dependent population (children and elderly). This window presents a massive opportunity for economic acceleration, provided there is adequate investment in education, healthcare, and job creation.

However, this dividend will not last forever. By the middle of the century, the proportion of elderly citizens will rise sharply, shifting the national focus from generating employment to providing elderly care and healthcare infrastructure. The decisions made today regarding education and skills training will determine whether this dividend is fully realized.
