India's Changing Population: The Demographic Dividend & Regional Transition

Beneath the headline of the world's most populous nation lies a historic demographic inflection point: sub-replacement fertility, stark regional divergence, and a closing demographic window.

IM
IndiaMetrix Research Desk
Published: August 2026 • Census & NFHS-5 Benchmarks • 6 min read

Key Demographic Takeaways

  • National Total Fertility Rate (TFR) has dropped to 2.0 (NFHS-5), below the replacement benchmark of 2.1 children per woman.
  • Regional dichotomy: Northern plains (Bihar TFR 2.98, UP 2.35) retain young medians, while southern states (Tamil Nadu TFR 1.42, Kerala 1.57) are entering advanced aging.
  • Demographic dividend window: India's working-age share peaks between 2020 and 2045, representing the defining window to achieve high-income status.
  • Internal migration: 100+ million domestic migrants drive urbanization in western and southern industrial hubs, creating critical inter-state labor linkages.

The Long Arc of Population Growth

In 1951, shortly after Independence, India counted 361.1 million citizens. Today, the nation stands as the most populous country on Earth, with a registered population exceeding 1.45 billion. This four-fold expansion was not the outcome of runaway birth rates, but rather one of the greatest triumphs in public health history: life expectancy nearly doubled from 32.1 years in 1951 to over 70.8 years today, alongside plummeting infant mortality rates.

Contrary to obsolete Malthusian rhetoric, India's annual population growth rate has dropped from a peak of 2.2% per year in the 1970s and 1980s to under 0.8% today. Over 31 out of 36 States and Union Territories have witnessed fertility rates decline below the replacement threshold, marking the beginning of the final chapter in India's demographic transition.

India is experiencing two opposing demographic realities simultaneously. Peninsular states like Kerala (TFR 1.57), Tamil Nadu (TFR 1.42), Andhra Pradesh (TFR 1.68), and Karnataka (TFR 1.70) achieved replacement fertility decades ago. Today, their demographic pyramids resemble southern European nations, characterized by shrinking school enrollments, an expanding elderly dependency ratio, and labor shortfalls in agriculture and construction.

Conversely, the heartland states of Bihar (TFR 2.98), Uttar Pradesh (TFR 2.35), and Madhya Pradesh (TFR 2.00) possess median ages below 24 years. Over 45% of India's population under 15 years old resides in these states. Consequently, internal economic migration has surged: millions of workers from northern and eastern states have moved to Chennai, Bengaluru, Mumbai, and Surat to staff factories, logistics centers, and retail industries.

The Closing Window: Capitalizing on the Demographic Dividend

Economists define a demographic dividend as the window during which the ratio of the working-age population (ages 15 to 64) is at its peak relative to young and elderly dependents. In India, this dependency ratio reached a historic low of under 48% around 2020 and will remain favorable until roughly 2045-2050. After 2050, as our calculations on the Demographic Target Projector illustrate, the elderly dependency ratio will rise rapidly.

East Asian economies like South Korea, Taiwan, and China leveraged their demographic dividends to leap from developing agrarian economies to industrial high-income powers within a single generation. India has approximately twenty years remaining to replicate this feat by building world-class manufacturing employment, accelerating female labor force participation (which currently lags at ~37%), and equipping youth with technical vocational skills.

Frequently Asked Questions on India's Population

Has India's fertility rate dropped below replacement level?

Yes. According to the National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5), India's national TFR dropped to 2.0 children per woman, which is below the replacement level of 2.1 required for a population to maintain its size without immigration.

When is India's total population expected to peak?

United Nations demographic projections indicate India's population will peak at approximately 1.68 to 1.70 billion around the early 2060s, before experiencing gradual demographic decline toward the turn of the 22nd century.
